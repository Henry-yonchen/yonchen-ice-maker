Shipping an Ice Machine from China

What happens between placing an order and the machine arriving: production time, container choices, documents, and the delays that are predictable if you plan for them.

The sequence, in order

  1. Specification confirmed in writing. Model, capacity, voltage, plug type, refrigerant, panel and packaging. This is the step that causes most later disputes — a verbal agreement on "220V" is not a specification.
  2. Certification arranged if needed. CE and ETL sit with the product. Market-specific schemes — SASO, SAA, SABS, KC, PSE — are arranged per order and add time. Start this before production, not after.
  3. Deposit and production. 25–35 days for bulk. Samples in 7–10 days if you want to see the unit before committing.
  4. Inspection. Either your own inspector, a third party, or a factory test report with photos and a running video. Ask which one you are getting before you assume.
  5. Booking and loading. From Ningbo or Shanghai. Loading plan depends on model size and packing method.
  6. Documents and sailing. Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin where required.

Container or LCL

Countertop and small commercial machines pack densely and usually justify a full container once you are ordering in volume. Industrial units — the 1–3 tonne flake machines — occupy a large share of a container each, so the freight cost per unit is a different calculation entirely.

For a first order, LCL is common. The trade-off is handling: an LCL consignment is consolidated and deconsolidated, which means more physical handling than a sealed container. If the machines are heavy or the cartons are not designed for it, that matters.

FAQ

Questions

How long does production take?

Samples ship in 7–10 days. Bulk production is typically 25–35 days depending on order size and customisation — OEM work with custom panels or non-standard voltage sits at the longer end. Shipping time is additional and depends on the route.

Should I buy FOB or CIF?

FOB gives you control and visibility of freight cost, and is normal once you have a forwarder. CIF is simpler for a first order because one party arranges everything, but the freight component is opaque. DDP suits buyers who want a landed cost and no customs involvement.

What documents come with the shipment?

Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and a certificate of origin where the destination requires one. Certification documents for the destination market — CE, ETL, SASO and similar — travel with the order when they have been arranged.

Planning a first container?

Tell us the models and the destination port — we will come back with production timing and a loading plan.

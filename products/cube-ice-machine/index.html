What this series is for

Cube and crescent ice are the default for food service: hard, clear, slow melting, and shaped to stack in a glass without displacing too much liquid. CIM is the series that covers every scale of that demand in one product family.

Because 28 models share components, a distributor can stock one spare-parts set and serve small cafés through to hotel kitchens and fast-food chains. Output scales in small steps, so buyers do not over-buy capacity.

Buying Questions

Cube and Crescent Ice Machines — questions buyers ask

Crescent or cube — which sells better?

Cube is the general-purpose default and the easier stock item. Crescent is specified by fast-food and beverage operators because the shape packs well in cups. Carrying both shapes in the 60–120 kg band usually covers most enquiries.

How do I size a machine for a restaurant?

Estimate 0.5–1 kg of ice per cover for a full-service restaurant, then add 20% headroom for summer peaks. Match that daily figure to the model's 24h output, not to bin capacity.

What is the largest model in this series?

1200 kg/24h. Above that, projects are usually served by the FIM series for flake ice or by multiple CIM units staged together.

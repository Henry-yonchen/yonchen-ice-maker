Ice Machines for Supermarket and Retail Display

Retail display ice has two jobs: keep the product cold, and keep the counter looking like it was just dressed. Both fail if the machine is sized on the display bed instead of the daily top-up.

Display beds are topped up, not filled once

Ice on an open bed melts through the trading day. Staff add ice two or three times to keep the bed dressed, so the daily requirement is the sum of those top-ups, not the volume of the bed at 8 a.m.

Nugget ice is the usual choice for seafood and meat because it is soft, does not puncture packaging, and settles around product. Flake is used where the product is iced directly — whole fish, shellfish, and back-of-house prep.

A common planning figure is 1.5–2 times the visible bed volume per shift. It is a starting point for sizing, not a specification: ambient temperature, bed depth and how often staff dress the counter all move it.

What to check before choosing

  • Size to top-up volume across the day; the bed volume alone will undersize the machine.
  • Nugget for packaged seafood and meat; flake where product is iced directly.
  • Locate the machine close to the counter — carrying ice across a sales floor is the usual reason beds go undressed.
  • Consider a second smaller unit for peak weekends rather than one oversized machine running at part load all week.
Sizing is decided by the duty cycle, the ambient temperature and how the ice is used — not by floor area alone. Send the operating pattern and we will shortlist two or three models instead of quoting the whole range.

Questions

Common questions for this application

How much ice does a seafood display counter need per day?

Plan on 1.5–2 times the bed volume per shift as a starting estimate, then confirm against actual top-up practice for a week. A 3 m bed typically lands in the 300–600 kg/24h range across a full day.

Will nugget ice damage packaged product?

No — nugget ice is soft and does not have the sharp edges of cube, so it does not puncture vacuum packs or trays. That is one reason it is standard on seafood counters.

Can the same machine serve seafood and produce?

It can, but the two departments usually run different hours and different top-up patterns. Separate units sized to each counter are easier to run than one shared machine.

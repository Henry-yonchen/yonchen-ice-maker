Flake Ice Machines for Concrete Cooling

How ice is used in a batching plant

Ice replaces part of the mixing water. Because it absorbs latent heat as it melts, a relatively small mass of ice removes far more heat than the same mass of cold water — which is why ice dosing is the standard lever for placement temperature in hot weather.

The quantity is not a fixed ratio: it depends on the mix design, the cementitious content, the aggregate temperature and the placement temperature the project specifies. Sizing starts from the batching plant's cycle time and the coldest water the plant can supply.

Flake ice is used rather than block or cube because it melts quickly and disperses through the mix. Sub-cooled flake at −5 °C to −8 °C also carries more cooling capacity per kilogram than wet, slushy ice.

What to check before choosing

  • Rate matters more than daily total: the machine has to deliver ice at the pace the batching cycle consumes it.
  • Rated output falls as ambient temperature rises — plan for the hottest month on site, not the annual average.
  • Ice must be conveyed and stored without melting; a machine that makes 3 tonnes a day is useless if the storage silo loses it before dosing.
  • In dusty plant environments, condenser cleaning access is a design requirement, not an afterthought.
Sizing is decided by the duty cycle, the ambient temperature and how the ice is used — not by floor area alone. Send the operating pattern and we will shortlist two or three models instead of quoting the whole range.

Questions

Common questions for this application

How much ice does a cubic metre of concrete need?

There is no universal figure — it follows from the mix design and the target placement temperature, and it is normally calculated by the project's concrete technologist. We size the machine once that ice demand per batch and the cycle time are known.

Why flake ice instead of block ice?

Flake disperses through the mix and melts fast enough to act before setting begins. Block ice has to be crushed first, which adds a step and equipment.

Can the machine run on a generator at a remote site?

It can, but the electrical supply has to be specified with the machine — starting current on the compressor is the constraint, not running load. Tell us the site supply when enquiring.

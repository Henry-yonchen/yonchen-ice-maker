Ice Machines for Seafood Processing and Display

Fish is chilled by contact, so the shape of the ice matters more than the tonnage. Flake and nugget ice cover the whole surface of the product without puncturing skin — which is why processors and fish counters specify them instead of cube.

Why flake and nugget ice

Flake ice is thin, dry and mouldable. It packs around a whole fish or a fillet and stays in contact with the surface instead of sitting in gaps the way cube does. More contact area means faster chilling in the first hours after catch, which is the window that decides shelf life.

Nugget ice works the same way on a display counter. It is soft, so it does not bruise the product, and it holds its shape in a bed for longer than crushed ice, which keeps the counter looking saleable through a trading day.

On the flake side, our FIM machines produce sub-cooled flake at −5 °C to −8 °C from a 304 stainless steel evaporator — the colder and drier the flake, the less it collapses into slush on the fish.

What to check before choosing

  • Match output to the daily catch or display turnover, not to the size of the cold room — an undersized machine runs continuously and wears faster.
  • In a wet, salty environment, specify corrosion protection for the frame and controls; a land-based plant and a fishing port are not the same installation.
  • For display counters, allow for topping up through the day: a bed is replenished rather than filled once.
  • Drainage and floor slope matter as much as the machine — meltwater pooling around the display is a food-safety problem, not just a cosmetic one.
Sizing is decided by the duty cycle, the ambient temperature and how the ice is used — not by floor area alone. Send the operating pattern and we will shortlist two or three models instead of quoting the whole range.

Questions

Common questions for this application

Flake ice or nugget ice for fish?

Flake for whole fish, fillets and transport — it moulds to the shape and chills fastest. Nugget for display beds and service counters, where a soft, slow-collapsing bed looks better for longer.

Can one machine serve both processing and display?

Usually not well. Processing needs cold, dry flake at volume; display needs a bed that stays presentable. Most operations run a larger flake machine at the back and a smaller unit at the counter.

What capacity should a fish counter use?

Size for the daily top-up volume rather than the visible bed. A counter that is topped up three times a day needs the machine's 24h output to exceed the total ice added across those top-ups.

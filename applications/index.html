Six ways buyers use ice

Each application has its own sizing logic — start from the operating pattern, not the machine.

FIM + GIM Series

Seafood Processing

Fish is chilled by contact, so the shape of the ice matters more than the tonnage. Flake and nugget ice cover the whole surface of the product without puncturing skin — which is why processors and fish counters specify them instead of cube.

200–3000 kg/24h
FIM Series

Concrete Cooling

In hot climates and mass pours, flake ice is dosed into the mix to hold down concrete temperature. The machine is a process component, so reliability and output at ambient temperature matter more than appearance.

500–3000 kg/24h
CIM + SIM Series

Hotel & Restaurant

A hotel needs ice in several places at once: the bar, the banquet kitchen, room-service pantries and the buffet. The mistake is buying one large machine for the whole property instead of sizing each point of use.

25–1200 kg/24h
SIM + CIM Series

Café & Bar

Café and bar ice is a throughput problem, not a storage problem. What matters is how fast the machine refills the bin during service, not how much the bin holds at opening time.

25–200 kg/24h
GIM + FIM Series

Supermarket & Retail Display

Retail display ice has two jobs: keep the product cold, and keep the counter looking like it was just dressed. Both fail if the machine is sized on the display bed instead of the daily top-up.

100–3000 kg/24h
GIM + CIM Series

Beverage Chain

A beverage chain buys the same machine for every store, so the decision is made once and repeated. Standardisation, spare-parts commonality and continuous duty matter more than the lowest unit price.

100–1200 kg/24h

Why application matters more than capacity

Two buyers can need the same 500 kg/24h output and still need different machines. A seafood processor wants cold, dry flake that moulds to the product. A supermarket wants a soft bed that still looks dressed at 6 p.m. A beverage chain wants a machine that recovers between peak hours without stopping.

That is why our series pages describe the product, and these pages describe the job. If you already know the capacity, the application page tells you which series to look at. If you already know the application, it tells you what capacity to plan for.

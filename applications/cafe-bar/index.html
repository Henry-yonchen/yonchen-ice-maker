Ice Machines for Cafés and Bars

Café and bar ice is a throughput problem, not a storage problem. What matters is how fast the machine refills the bin during service, not how much the bin holds at opening time.

Throughput over bin size

A bar can empty a bin in a busy evening and then wait hours for it to refill. Recovery rate — kilograms per hour — is the number to compare, which is why we quote daily output and cycle time together.

Air-cooled machines are simpler to install and need no condenser water, but they reject heat into the room. In a small, hot back-bar that raises the load on the air conditioning; water-cooled units handle it better where a drain is available.

For coffee shops, most demand is iced drinks in a short afternoon window. A 50–100 kg/24h self-contained unit covers a typical single-site café; a second outlet or a summer terrace usually needs its own machine.

What to check before choosing

  • Compare recovery rate, not just 24h output — the bin empties before the day ends.
  • Measure the space under the counter and the door swing before choosing a model.
  • In a hot back-bar, prefer water cooling if a drain exists; otherwise plan ventilation for the air-cooled unit.
  • Spare bin and scoop hygiene is a health-inspection item — choose a model with a removable, cleanable bin liner.
Sizing is decided by the duty cycle, the ambient temperature and how the ice is used — not by floor area alone. Send the operating pattern and we will shortlist two or three models instead of quoting the whole range.

Questions

Common questions for this application

What size machine does a 40-seat café need?

Usually 50–80 kg/24h for an espresso-led café with iced drinks, moving to 100 kg/24h if there is a summer terrace or a takeaway window. Peak-hour recovery is what decides between the two.

Can a bar use a domestic countertop machine?

For garnish ice in a very small venue, yes. For a working bar well it will not keep up — domestic units are built for household duty cycles.

Air cooled or water cooled?

Air cooled unless the machine sits in a hot, enclosed back-bar with no ventilation. Water cooled removes heat through the drain but needs a permanent water connection.
